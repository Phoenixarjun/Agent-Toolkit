# Problem Framing

A useful problem statement describes an undesirable current condition and the affected party without embedding a preferred implementation.

## Input classification

**Observed problem**  
Concrete pain, inefficiency, risk, unmet need, or undesirable outcome is identifiable.

**Symptom**  
Something is wrong, but the underlying cause is uncertain.

**Solution disguised as a problem**  
The statement prescribes an implementation without establishing why it is needed.

**Aspiration**  
The statement expresses improvement without a concrete current problem.

**Insufficient**  
The statement lacks enough substance to evaluate.

## Evidence labels

- **FACT** — supported by direct or reliable evidence
- **ASSUMPTION** — believed but not yet established
- **UNKNOWN** — information not yet available
- **CONSTRAINT** — a condition the solution must respect
- **DECISION** — a deliberate choice, not a discovered fact

Do not promote an assumption to fact because it sounds plausible.

## Readiness

A problem is sufficiently framed when exploration can answer:
- who is affected
- what undesirable condition exists
- why it matters
- what better outcome is sought
- what hard constraints already exist
- what remains assumption or unknown

If a material gap remains, use GrillMe with the smallest sufficient mode rather than inventing the answer.
