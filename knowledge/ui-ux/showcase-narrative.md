# Product Showcase Narrative and Proof

Load only when a product-marketing, launch or showcase request requires content architecture, defensible proof, visitor-action sequencing or redesign references. This is supporting knowledge, not a workflow, skill or marketing-copy generator.

## Start with the product and the visitor decision

A homepage is not a table of product features. Its job is to help a visitor judge whether the product solves their problem, whether the claims are credible, and what next step is appropriate. Establish:

```text
Product fact: What can the product demonstrably do?
Visitor job: Why is this audience here now?
Differentiator: What choice becomes different because of this product?
Proof: What existing asset can justify that difference?
Action: What sensible step should the visitor take next?
```

Separate observed truth, user-provided assertions, desired future features and marketing hypotheses. A page must not make the latter look independently verified.

## Let the proof choose the composition

Choose the dominant hero/proof format from assets and user questions, not from a default marketing template.

| Available proof | Useful form | Failure to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Actual CLI/SDK workflow | Runnable example, truthful terminal excerpt, interactive code/result | Decorative fake terminal that proves nothing |
| Working product interface | Real product interaction, annotated screen, task progression | Invented dashboards and fake metrics |
| System behavior | Source-grounded diagram, event/decision sequence, real output | Abstract network imagery detached from behavior |
| Physical object | Authentic photography, scale/detail shots, optional inspection | Synthetic depictions misleading about size or finish |
| Trusted service and credentials | Verifiable people, process, credentials, transparent terms | Unsupported badges, claims and testimonial logos |
| No usable evidence yet | Plain-language explanation and explicit missing-proof plan | Fabricating proof to support a fashionable layout |

A strong design may be typographic and restrained. A flashy design may be appropriate when the actual product experience warrants it. Neither choice is intrinsically better.

## Section and page narrative selection

Structure sections by distinct visitor questions, not stock headings. A section should contribute a new answer, proof, decision or route. The page can use different orders depending on whether visitors arrive to explore, compare, evaluate trust, read documentation or take an immediate action.

For each proposed section establish:

```text
visitor_question
one_message
supporting_proof_or_gap
visual_mechanism
next_action_or_transition
```

Use clear action labels that predict what happens next. Distinguish primary adoption actions from secondary investigation links. A GitHub/quickstart CTA may be stronger for a public developer tool than a forced demo form; consult context. Do not assert that a CTA arrangement improves conversion absent experiment data.

Feature-to-benefit translation must preserve mechanism: what the feature actually does, for whom, and what job it helps. Avoid unsupported numeric benefits or claims of universal suitability.

## Art direction and story must influence each other

Do not permanently freeze copy before layout exploration. The available proof may justify reducing text, splitting one overloaded section or using a sequence instead of a comparison grid. Revisit only the affected content and layout decision, not the underlying verified product facts.

Distinct creative directions should diverge in:

1. **Proof mechanism:** What real evidence leads the page?
2. **Composition and narrative rhythm:** How does the visitor read/act through it?
3. **Visual character:** What typographic, color, imagery, shape or interaction choices express the product?

Three palette swaps are one direction, not three. A design direction is valid only if it preserves comprehensibility, accessibility and actual product constraints.

## Scope and originality

Common components, fonts, libraries and icons are acceptable when contextually earned. A different icon library alone does not create product identity. A truly signature interaction or graphic should originate from the product's material, mechanism, domain or visitor task. Use existing `anti-generic-ui.md` for generic-pattern diagnosis and replacement, not this file.

## Website review evidence

Inspect in this order within every authorized page:

1. **Section:** actual copy, real proof, CTA, local visitor job and observed issue.
2. **Layout:** relation among sections, hierarchy, responsive composition, rhythm and motion/imagery fit.
3. **Page:** narrative, trust development, action path and navigation to next pages.

A broad complaint with no exact page/section/layout evidence is weak. Distinguish observed defects from testable hypotheses, preferences and inaccessible states. Preserve an existing brand if the evidence supports surgical improvements.

## Generated or retrieved visual references

Optional conceptual images help communicate an approved direction, not validate code. Distinguish retrieved reference, generated concept, editable prototype, implemented screenshot and runtime-tested experience. Keep credit/licensing/provenance for retrieved assets. Do not imitate a competitor's distinctive visual design or invent a live product screenshot. If image generation is unavailable or inappropriate, a dimensioned wireframe and precise visual specification are sufficient.

## Human validation boundary

Simulated audience evaluations surface comprehension hypotheses. They do not prove appeal, purchase intent, market demand or conversion uplift. Test real visitors when important decisions depend on those outcomes.
