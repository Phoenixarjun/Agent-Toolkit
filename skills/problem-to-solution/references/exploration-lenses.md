# Exploration Lenses

Use these only when the current exploration needs broader coverage or sharper comparison. They are prompts for reasoning, not a checklist.

## Divergence

Consider whether the problem could be improved by:
- removing a step
- changing ownership or responsibility
- changing timing
- changing incentives
- changing the interaction channel
- using existing infrastructure differently
- automating only the bottleneck
- replacing coordination with a simpler mechanism
- adding software
- adding hardware
- combining physical and digital mechanisms
- changing policy or governance
- changing the business model
- accepting the problem while reducing its impact

## Stakeholders

Distinguish roles when relevant:
- user
- buyer
- payer
- operator
- administrator
- approver
- maintainer
- regulator
- integration partner
- affected non-user
- beneficiary

A candidate can create value for one role while creating resistance or cost for another.

## Adoption

Ask:
- what behavior must change
- who must approve the change
- what migration is required
- whether adoption can be incremental
- who loses convenience, control, revenue, or status if it succeeds

## Feasibility

Reason from requirements before products or technologies:
- workload
- scale
- latency
- consistency
- availability
- data shape and retention
- integration constraints
- deployment environment
- safety and security
- team and operational maturity

## Risk

Use only domain-relevant risks:
- safety
- privacy
- security
- regulation
- reliability
- abuse
- operational burden
- dependency concentration
- vendor lock-in
- migration risk
- irreversible capital cost

## Experimentability

Prefer the cheapest experiment capable of invalidating an important assumption:
- manual concierge test
- prototype
- simulation
- shadow mode
- single-team or single-location pilot
- integration spike
- demand test
- operational trial

Test the assumption most capable of killing the candidate, not merely whether something can be built.
