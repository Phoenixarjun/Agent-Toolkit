# Evidence and substitution decision guide

Load only when competing alternatives, AI-wrapper exposure, quantified economics, disputed differentiation, or evidence quality require deeper judgment. This is a procedure, not market-domain knowledge or a checklist to execute in full.

## Preserve provenance

For each claim that drives the verdict, record the proposition, evidence category, source identity/locator, observed period, verification or access date, limitation, and alternative explanation. Distinguish:

- `OBSERVED`: real product data, customer action, interview statement, instrumented event, or user-supplied artifact; identify who observed it, when, and under what conditions. Statements from interviews are **reported opinions** until behavior is confirmed.
- `RESEARCHED`: attributable external source. Establish precisely what the source supports, including whether it is a competitor's own claim or an independently established result.
- `INFERRED`: reasoned implication that is not directly observed; include its premise and a plausible counterexample.
- `UNVALIDATED`: material hypothesis with no adequate evidence, or evidence that cannot be inspected.

Synthetic user personas, invented interviews, benchmark demos and model-generated estimates remain synthetic. Independent reviewers are not customers. No evidence from an unavailable website should be described as inspected. Multiple articles repeating one announcement are not independent corroboration. Recheck dates for current prices, features and market claims.

## Substitution test

Ask in order, stopping when enough is known:

1. **Job:** What outcome does the user hire this product to deliver, in the user's own workflow?
2. **Status quo:** How is the outcome achieved today, including doing nothing?
3. **Present substitutes:** Which concrete products, workflows, people or generic AI systems could provide an acceptably similar result?
4. **Switching cost:** What must a user do, pay, trust, change or give up to adopt this product? Compare against the substitute, not an imaginary empty market.
5. **Incumbent scenario:** If a provider offers the same headline feature, what enduring service, workflow, relationship, data access, taste, distribution, verification or integration advantage remains? Avoid treating this hypothetical as certain.
6. **Value reality:** Which claimed advantages have *actual customer evidence*? Which are untested marketing hypotheses?
7. **Decision:** Does the product still solve an outcome better enough, for its intended purpose, to justify its ongoing cost and risks?

Easy cloning of code does not necessarily equal easy replacement of user value. Conversely, "integrated", "AI-powered", "proprietary prompts", "agents", "personalized", and "enterprise-ready" are not differentiators without a demonstrated improvement for a defined user.

A commodity implementation can succeed through taste, fit, distribution, trust, constraints, community or low friction. Distinguish **current benefit** from **durable competitive advantage**. No product needs a permanent monopoly to be useful.

## Product-type adaptation

- **Personal tool:** Evaluate the creator's actual utility, time saved, delight, and maintenance cost. Competitive moat, market size and monetization are usually not applicable.
- **Open-source project:** Evaluate real use cases, user effort, integration, maintenance burden, community signals and alternatives. Revenue only if the owner chooses commercial goals.
- **Internal tool:** Evaluate workflow impact, user adoption, cost/risk avoided, support burden and organizational feasibility. External competitor rankings may be secondary.
- **Commercial product:** Evaluate buyer/user distinction, activation, retention, distribution, switching behavior, delivery cost, willingness to pay and substitute threats. Use real market evidence where possible.
- **Product feature:** Assess incremental effect on the existing product's core outcome, not whether the feature can become a standalone company.

These are examples of adaptation, not mutually exclusive rigid categories.

## Quantitative discipline

Identify units, period, denominator, data source and range before calculating. For a credible economic argument, separate:

- **Observed:** Measured conversion, cost, retention, usage or savings with stated scope.
- **Modeled:** Mathematical result based on disclosed inputs, with sensitivity to critical assumptions.
- **Forecast:** Projection supported by a stated demand basis but not yet observed.
- **Unknown:** Missing price, cost, adoption, or counterfactual behavior.

Do not turn "hours saved" directly into cash ROI without accounting for adoption, time utilization, actual baseline, quality differences and implementation/operating costs. Never generate plausible customer, competitor, TAM, CAGR, conversion, LTV or margin numbers as facts. If numbers are insufficient, give conditional break-even reasoning instead of fabricated precision.

## Falsification and evolution

Choose one smallest useful experiment by the **uncertainty it resolves**:

- Problem uncertain: observe real workaround frequency, impact and urgency.
- Value uncertain: run a representative user task with the product and current substitute; compare outcomes.
- Adoption uncertain: measure actual sign-up-to-activation or switching in a defined population.
- Payment uncertain: test a credible paid commitment, not only survey enthusiasm.
- Platform exposure uncertain: map value remaining after a realistic substitute feature arrives.
- Economics uncertain: measure actual delivery cost on representative workloads.

Every proposed experiment needs a decision rule: what result would justify proceeding, reconsidering, or stopping? Thresholds may be qualitative if numeric thresholds would be artificial. Name the evidence owner and a meaningful re-evaluation trigger. Do not run external experiments or contact customers without permission.

## Independent critique budget

Consider one fresh-context critique for investment, irreversible commitments, strong disagreement or fragile evidence. Challenge the strongest claims and look for a cheaper, simpler alternative. Do not spin up role-based buyer/investor/customer agents as substitutes for reality. Stop when further speculative critique adds no decision-changing evidence.
